Part 3: Know your Data
Suggested time: 10 minutes
Before drawing conclusions, investigate the dataset.
Find out:
How many rows and columns does it contain?
What are the column names?
Which columns contain numerical data, and which contain categorical data?
Are there any missing values?
What are the average, minimum, and maximum values of selected numerical columns?
You may use Pandas documentation, your previous learning materials, or other resources to figure out the necessary commands.

Part 4: Ask Your Own Questions
Suggested time: 10 minutes
Now comes the fun part!
Your team must formulate at least three questions that can be investigated using the dataset.
You may investigate study habits, attendance, academic performance, departments, participation, or any other variables that interest you.
For example:
Do students who study more tend to score higher?
Does average performance differ across departments?
Is attendance associated with examination scores?
Do students with different previous academic results perform differently?
Does class participation appear to be related to total scores?
These are only starting points. Try to choose questions that make your group's investigation distinctive.

Part 5: Data Exploration and Visualization (Suggested time: 30 minutes)
Question 3. Discuss as a group and write down your answers.
Why do we need to explore data before doing any machine learning task?
Why, and how, do we look for patterns in a dataset?
Does visualization (plotting the data) help? If so, how?
Possible charts include:
Bar chart: Compare average scores across departments.
Histogram: Investigate the distribution of examination marks.
Scatter plot: Explore the relationship between study hours and total score.
Box plot: Compare score distributions across participation levels.
Line plot: Explore scores across students or another meaningful ordered variable.
Heatmap: Explore correlations between numerical variables.
